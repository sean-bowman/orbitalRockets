## Overview

An annotated index of the standards this domain works to, with what each one is actually for. Short on purpose: these are the documents an environments engineer opens, not everything that exists.

---

## The derivation references

| Standard | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **NASA-HDBK-7005** | **The derivation reference.** How to get from flight data to a specification, including the statistics |
| IES-RP-DTE012 | Handbook for dynamic data acquisition and analysis. The measurement half |
| **NASA-HDBK-7004** | **Force limited vibration testing.** The defensible basis for notching |
| NASA-STD-5002 | Load analyses of spacecraft and payloads. The CLA process |

**NASA-HDBK-7005 is the one document to read** if the goal is to be able to derive and defend a level rather than accept one.

## Generic specifications

**These are what a programme uses before it has flight data, and using one is legitimate as long as it is labelled as chosen rather than derived.**

| Standard | Character |
|---|---|
| **GSFC-STD-7000 (GEVS)** | The most widely used general specification. 14.1 Grms qualification |
| **MIL-STD-1540** | Test requirements, and a minimum workmanship screen |
| MIL-STD-810 | A very broad set of environmental test methods, mostly terrestrial |
| ECSS-E-ST-10-03 | The European testing standard |

**GEVS is a specification and a philosophy.** Its qualification spectrum is the anchor most people compare against, and its structure, acceptance at MPE and qualification at +3 dB for twice the duration, is the margin policy most programmes adopt.

## Test requirements

| Standard | Scope |
|---|---|
| **MIL-STD-1540** | The classic launch and space vehicle test requirements |
| **SMC-S-016** | Its current successor for US government launch programmes |
| NASA-STD-7002 | Payload test requirements |
| ECSS-E-ST-10-03 | European equivalent |
| **NASA-STD-7001** | Payload vibroacoustic test criteria specifically |

**These define what tests are run and at what level**, and they are where the acceptance and qualification distinction is formalised.

## How they fit together

A component environment specification touches most of the list in a definite order:

| Step | Standard |
|---|---|
| **1. Decide whether data exists** | NASA-HDBK-7005 |
| **2a. If it does: derive** | NASA-HDBK-7005, IES-RP-DTE012 |
| **2b. If it does not: choose and label** | GSFC-STD-7000, MIL-STD-1540 |
| **3. Apply the margin policy** | GSFC-STD-7000 or MIL-STD-1540 |
| **4. Define the test** | NASA-STD-7001, NASA-STD-7003, MIL-STD-810 |
| **5. Justify any notching** | NASA-HDBK-7004 |
| 6. Feed the structural analysis | NASA-STD-5001, NASA-STD-5002 |

**Step 2b is the one that matters most in practice**, because most programmes are there. Choosing a generic specification is fine; failing to record that it was chosen is what causes the trouble later, when nobody can say how much margin the level contains.
