## NASA design criteria monographs

The **NASA SP-8000 series** was written in the late 1960s and early 1970s to capture the design practice of the Apollo era. They are dated in their materials and their computational methods and they remain the best single source on the physics and the design logic of liquid rocket fluid systems. They are freely available from NASA NTRS.

| Monograph | Subject |
|---|---|
| **SP-8080** | Liquid rocket pressure regulators, relief valves, check valves, burst disks and explosive valves |
| **SP-8087** | Liquid rocket engine fluid-cooled combustion chambers |
| **SP-8089** | Liquid rocket engine injectors |
| SP-8090 | Liquid rocket actuators and operators |
| SP-8094 | Liquid rocket valve components |
| SP-8097 | Liquid rocket valve assemblies |
| SP-8109 | Liquid rocket engine centrifugal flow turbopumps |
| SP-8113 | Liquid rocket engine combustion stabilization devices |
| SP-8119 | Liquid rocket disconnects, couplings, fittings, fixed joints and seals |
| SP-8123 | Liquid rocket lines, bellows, flexible hoses and filters |
| **SP-8080 (tank pressurization)** | Liquid rocket propellant tank pressurization |
| SP-8007 | Buckling of thin-walled circular cylinders |

**SP-8119 and SP-8123 are the two most directly relevant** to the subject matter of this library and are worth reading end to end.

## How to use standards

**Standards are not a substitute for understanding.** A design that meets every applicable standard and is wrong is still wrong; the standards codify accumulated experience about known failure modes, and they say nothing about the ones nobody has had yet.

**Know which regime you are in.** ASME B31.3 and AIAA S-080 are both legitimate and they are not interchangeable. Ground systems, flight systems, and manned systems have different governing documents and different factors, and a component qualified under one is not automatically acceptable under another.

**Check the revision.** Standards are revised, sometimes substantially. Cite the revision in the requirement.

**Read the scope section first.** Most misapplication comes from using a standard outside its stated scope: ISO 5167 outside its diameter and Reynolds number range, an elastomer specification outside its temperature range, a fitting standard outside its pressure class.

**Tailor deliberately and document it.** Programs tailor standards, and that is legitimate. What is not legitimate is tailoring by omission, where a requirement quietly does not get applied because nobody noticed it. Write the tailoring down.

**The propulsion-specific documents that repay reading in full:**

1. NASA SP-8119 and SP-8123 (fluid system components)
2. NASA SP-8080 (pressure control and tank pressurization)
3. ASTM G88 (oxygen system design)
4. Crane TP-410 (flow of fluids; not a standard but universally referenced)
5. AIAA S-080 (flight pressure hardware)
