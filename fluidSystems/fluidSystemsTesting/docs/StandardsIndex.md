## How to use them

**MIL-STD-1540 is the one to read end to end.** It carries the qualification and acceptance matrix, the margin policy, and the rationale for both, and most other documents in this list either feed it or elaborate one of its cells.

**Know which regime you are in.** ASME B31.3 governs ground piping and AIAA S-080 governs flight pressure hardware. They use different philosophies, different factors, and they are not interchangeable. A component qualified under one is not automatically acceptable under the other, and a test conducted to the wrong one closes nothing.

**Read the scope section first.** Most misapplication comes from using a standard outside its stated scope: ISO 5167 outside its Reynolds and diameter range, a leak test method outside its sensitivity range, an environmental method outside the article class it was written for.

**Cite the revision.** Standards are revised substantially. A requirement citing "MIL-STD-1540" without a revision letter is ambiguous.

**Tailor deliberately and write it down.** Every one of these documents expects tailoring. What none of them tolerate is tailoring by omission. See [TestCampaignPlanning.md](TestCampaignPlanning.md).

**The documents that repay reading in full:**

1. MIL-STD-1540 (test requirements and rationale)
2. AIAA S-080 (pressure hardware verification)
3. ISO/IEC Guide 98-3, the GUM (uncertainty, and it is shorter than it looks)
4. ASTM E432 (leak method selection, a genuinely useful decision guide)
5. NASA-HDBK-8739.18 (root cause analysis, for the day you need it)
