## Overview

A wax pattern is coated in ceramic slurry, the wax is melted out, and metal is poured into the resulting shell. It is the dominant aerospace casting route and it produces geometry that no other casting process approaches.

---

## The process

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Wax injection** | Patterns injected into a metal die, one per part |
| **Assembly** | Patterns attached to a wax sprue to form a tree |
| **Shell building** | Repeated dip in slurry and stucco, 6 to 10 coats, dried between |
| **Dewax** | Autoclave or flash fire. The wax is melted out |
| **Burnout** | Shell fired to burn residual wax and develop strength |
| **Pour** | Often preheated shell, sometimes in vacuum |
| **Knockout** | Shell broken off |
| **Cut off, finish, inspect** | |

**The wax die is the tooling and it is the cost.** A metal die to injection-mould wax is a real investment, and it is why investment casting is a quantity process with a 20 week lead time.

**Shell building is the slow step.** Each coat has to dry before the next, so a shell takes days.

**Vacuum pouring is standard for superalloys and titanium**, because those alloys pick up oxygen and nitrogen from air.

---

## What it achieves

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| **Minimum wall** | 1.5 mm, and 0.75 mm with care |
| **Tolerance** | DCTG 6, roughly IT11 |
| **Surface** | 3.2 um Ra as cast |
| Complexity | Very high, including internal passages |
| Buy-to-fly | 1.6 : 1 |

**The geometric freedom is the reason to use it.** Undercuts, thin webs, internal passages, integral bosses and fine detail all come out as cast, and a part that would be a fabrication of six pieces becomes one.

**The surface is good enough to leave as cast** in many places, which is unusual for a casting and it is a real cost saving.

## Cores

Internal passages need cores, and the core has to come out.

| Core | Removal |
|---|---|
| **Ceramic** | Leached out chemically, usually caustic |
| Soluble wax | Dissolved before shelling |
| Preformed shell | Broken out |

**Ceramic core leaching is slow and it is not always complete.** Residual core material in a cooling passage is a real defect and it is inspected for, usually by flow test and by radiography.

**Core position tolerance sets the wall thickness tolerance.** A core that shifts by half a millimetre gives a wall that is half a millimetre thin on one side and thick on the other, and core shift is the commonest dimensional nonconformance in a cored casting.

## Directional and single crystal

**The high end of the process**, and it exists because grain boundaries are the weak point at high temperature.

| Structure | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Equiaxed** | Conventional pour | Baseline |
| **Directionally solidified** | Withdrawn slowly from a hot zone; grains grow along the axis | No transverse grain boundaries |
| **Single crystal** | A helical grain selector passes one grain | **No grain boundaries at all** |

**Turbine blades are the application**, and the progression from equiaxed to DS to single crystal is worth perhaps 50 K of turbine inlet temperature each step.

**Single crystal castings have no grain boundaries**, so they have no grain boundary creep, no grain boundary oxidation and no grain boundary strengthening elements. The alloys are formulated differently as a result, with the boron and carbon that would strengthen boundaries removed.

**It is a specialist supply chain** with very long lead times, and it is included here as context rather than as something most programmes will use.

## Design rules of thumb

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Minimum wall | 1.5 mm |
| Tolerance | DCTG 6 |
| As-cast surface | 3.2 um Ra, often usable |
| Buy-to-fly | 1.6 : 1 |
| Lead time | 20 weeks, dominated by the wax die |
| Vacuum pour for superalloys and titanium | Oxygen pickup |
| Core shift is the commonest nonconformance | It sets the wall tolerance |
| Verify core removal | Flow test and radiography |

## Failure modes

**Core shift.** A thin wall on one side.

**Residual ceramic core.** In a cooling passage, and it blocks it.

**Shell cracking during pour.** Metal escapes, and it is a scrapped tree.

**Insufficient shell coats.** The shell fails under the metal head.

**Wax die tolerance not accounted.** Every pattern is the same wrong shape.

**Titanium poured in air.** Alpha case on every surface.
