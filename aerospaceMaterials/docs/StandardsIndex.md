## How to use them

**MMPDS is the one to have.** It carries the allowables, the statistical method, the temperature curves and the specification cross references for every metal used in aerospace. Most questions this domain asks are answered in it.

**Know the difference between a specification and a handbook.** AMS 4911 tells you what Ti-6Al-4V sheet must be; MMPDS tells you what you may design to. They are not interchangeable and quoting one where the other is required is a review finding.

**Cite the revision.** These documents are revised substantially. MMPDS-18 and MIL-HDBK-5J do not carry the same numbers, and a requirement citing an unrevisioned standard is ambiguous.

**Check the product form and the thickness range.** An allowable is specific to both, and applying a sheet allowable to a 100 mm plate is a real error that looks like a lookup.

**AMS and EN specifications are not interchangeable.** They may cover nominally the same alloy with different composition limits, different testing and different acceptance. A cross-reference table is a starting point for an investigation, not a substitute for one.

**The documents that repay reading in full:**

1. MMPDS Chapter 9 (the statistical method, and it is shorter than it looks)
2. NASA-STD-6016 (materials and processes requirements, the integrating document)
3. NASA-STD-5019 (fracture control, and it will change how you size pressure vessels)
4. MSFC-SPEC-522 (stress corrosion, the alloy and temper tables)
5. AMS 2750 (pyrometry, because every heat treat certification rests on it)
